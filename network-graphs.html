Overview

Understanding a company's risk often means looking beyond the company itself. Relevant information can sit within a network of shareholders, beneficial owners, subsidiaries, directors, and other connected entities.

Due to confidentiality, this case study uses publicly available product information and simplified descriptions of my work. It does not include customer data, proprietary methodology, or unreleased product designs.

My role

I designed the network-graph experience within Maxsight and contributed to the connected Ownership and Risk Indicators chapters. The work helped transform complex corporate relationships and risk signals into a clearer investigation experience for compliance teams.

The challenge

Ownership structures are rarely simple. A company may be linked to many organisations and individuals, with relationships spanning multiple levels of ownership and control.

Users needed to understand who owns or controls an entity, how companies and people are connected, where potential sanctions or financial-crime exposure exists, and which relationships warranted further investigation. The challenge was to make a large and complex network understandable without removing the detail needed for due diligence.

Designing for investigation

The graph was not designed as a visualisation for its own sake. It needed to support a real investigation workflow.

I focused on helping users move between three levels of understanding: network overview → relevant connection → supporting evidence. At a high level, users can understand the shape of an ownership network. From there, they can identify a relationship or flagged connection, then move into detailed information to support an investigation.

Connecting ownership and risk

The Ownership chapter provides the structural context: shareholders, beneficial owners, subsidiaries, and other forms of company control.

Risk Indicators build on that structure by helping users understand potential direct or indirect exposure to sanctioned or financially criminal parties. The design needed to make these layers work together, so users could see not only that risk existed, but how it was connected to the focal company.

Design principles

Start with context — Help users orient themselves within a complex network before asking them to assess individual details.

Reveal complexity progressively — Keep the initial experience understandable while allowing deeper investigation where required.

Connect signals to evidence — Make it clear how a flagged relationship relates to the company and where users can find supporting information.

Outcome

The resulting experience supports a more visual, structured way to investigate company relationships and potential risk exposure within Maxsight. My work helped establish a framework that connects ownership data, network relationships, and risk indicators in a way that supports clearer compliance investigations.

Maxsight's public documentation describes the Network graph as a visual map of ownership and control, covering indirect ownership, beneficial owners, and global ultimate owners. Its Risk Indicators experience uses network and firmographic views to help uncover direct and indirect exposure to sanctions and financial crime.
